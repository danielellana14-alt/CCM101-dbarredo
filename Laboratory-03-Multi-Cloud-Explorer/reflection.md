# Reflection

Throughout this mission, I explored and compared Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform (GCP). Among the three platforms, Google Cloud Platform impressed me the most because of its strong capabilities in artificial intelligence, machine learning, Kubernetes, and global infrastructure. Services such as Vertex AI and Google Kubernetes Engine demonstrate how the platform can support modern and highly scalable applications.

AWS, Azure, and GCP have many similarities. All three provide virtual machines, object storage, databases, identity and access management, networking, and Kubernetes services. They also support scalable infrastructure and pay-as-you-go cloud computing models. Although their services have different names and features, they can generally provide equivalent solutions for many common business requirements.

Business requirements have a major influence on cloud platform selection. A startup with a limited budget may prioritize cost and scalability, while a university that already depends on Microsoft technologies may benefit more from Azure. An AI research company may prioritize machine learning and GPU capabilities, while a global e-commerce company may focus on availability, performance, and automatic scaling. This shows that there is no single cloud provider that is always the best choice for every organization.

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me that technical decisions should be connected to business objectives. It is important to evaluate the customer's existing infrastructure, budget, workload, scalability requirements, and future plans before recommending a platform.

My GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio also improved during this mission. I learned how to organize research and documentation using Markdown, compare cloud services, record Linux system information, and provide screenshots as evidence. Overall, the mission strengthened my understanding of multi-cloud computing and improved my ability to make cloud recommendations based on real business requirements.
